# Raven OS — Subscriber Account Lifecycle and Delegated Account Generation Decision
**Date:** 2026-11-05  
**Status:** User-approved product/architecture decision; implementation not implied  
**Repository branch:** `rescue/unified-arena-integration-2026-11-03`

## 1. Decision and ownership

Raven OS is the authoritative, independent owner of its subscriber identity and access domain. Raven centrally owns and governs:
- School and individual subscriber accounts.
- User identities, school memberships, and account relationships.
- Subscription entitlements and account-generation quotas.
- Roles, permissions, and authorization policies.

Raven identity and school records are independent of the government platforms Noor and Madrasati. Any future integrations with those platforms are separate, explicitly authorized workflows; they do not become Raven's identity provider or source of truth.

## 2. School subscriber account

After a school subscription is activated, the school principal/authorized school administrator may generate accounts within that school's own Raven tenant:
- **Teacher accounts:** generated by the school administrator for the school's teachers.
- **Student accounts:** generated by the school administrator for the school's students.
- **Family access:** the family is not a separate additional account in this decision; it uses/relates to the student's account, with the appropriate permitted access.

Generated accounts remain centrally governed Raven identities and are scoped to the issuing school. A school administrator must not be able to create accounts in, view, or administer another school's tenant.

## 3. Independent individual subscriber

An individual who subscribes independently activates their own Raven account and may generate a quota of subordinate accounts according to the selected subscription plan. The quota is plan-defined and may vary (for example, two, three, five, or another configured quantity). No single fixed quota is established by this decision.

The subscription entitlement, not a client-side control, must determine the allowed quantity. The server must enforce quota limits and ownership boundaries.

## 4. Single sign-in entry and destination routing

Raven has one sign-in entry point for the platform. After authentication, the system resolves the user's active account context, subscription entitlement, school membership (if any), role, and permissions, then routes the user to the appropriate authorized section/workspace.

There must not be separate public login gateways for school, teacher, student/family, or individual subscribers. A single entry point does not mean all users receive the same destination or permissions.

## 5. Security and lifecycle requirements

The eventual implementation must distinguish these concepts rather than treating account generation as a single unchecked operation:
1. Identity/account creation.
2. Invitation or credential/bootstrap issuance.
3. Account activation.
4. Membership and tenant association.
5. Role assignment.
6. Permission evaluation and enforcement.

The school administrator's delegated authority is limited to the school's tenant and subscription entitlements. An individual subscriber's delegated authority is limited to their own subscription and generated accounts. All authorization and quota checks must be enforced server-side; hiding UI controls is not security.

Account generation and administrative changes should be auditable. Avoid exposing credentials in logs or bulk-generation results; use a secure activation/credential-delivery flow when separately designed and approved.

Family access must be designed so that it does not silently grant a family member unrestricted access to a student's account. The precise family-member identity/session model and consent/guardian relationships remain to be specified before implementation.

## 6. Explicit non-decisions

This decision does **not** select or authorize:
- An identity/authentication provider, database, ORM, hosting change, or file-storage provider.
- A specific credential, invitation, password-reset, MFA, or account-recovery mechanism.
- The exact subscription tiers, quota values, overage behavior, or billing/payment integration.
- The detailed family/guardian access model.
- Automatic account generation or bulk import from Noor or Madrasati.
- Any direct integration or automated write operation against Noor or Madrasati.
- Production implementation, migrations, live writes, or deployment.

These require separate design decisions and explicit approval.

## 7. Acceptance criteria for a future implementation

- One sign-in entry point serves all subscriber types.
- Post-authentication routing is based on server-verified membership, entitlement, role, and permissions.
- An activated school subscription enables only authorized school administrators to generate teacher and student accounts within that school.
- Generated accounts are associated with the correct school tenant and cannot cross tenant boundaries.
- Family access is tied to the student's account relationship, with narrowly scoped permissions; it is not treated as an unrelated extra student account.
- Individual subscribers can generate only the number of accounts allowed by their active plan.
- Quota and authorization enforcement occurs server-side and is covered by negative tests.
- Account creation, activation, membership, role changes, and generation actions are auditable.
- Noor and Madrasati remain external systems; any integration is separately authorized and cannot replace Raven's identity or data ownership.

## 8. Governance note

This document records the user's approved ownership and account-generation model. It is a product/architecture decision, not evidence that authentication, account generation, billing, family access, or integrations are already implemented or production-ready.
